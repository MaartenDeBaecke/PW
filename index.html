Learning-based optimal control · from my MSc thesis

  • An Integrated Architecture for Data-Efficient Real-Time Neural Imitation of Nonlinear Time-Optimal Model Predictive Control Maarten De Baecke, Flavia Sofia Acerbo, Andrea Giusti, Wilm Decré In preparation

    Nonlinear time-optimal model predictive control (MPC) enables highly performant control of constrained systems, but its computational cost prohibits real-time use on fast dynamical systems. Neural network imitation of the MPC control law can reduce online computational costs, yet existing work mostly targets quadratic-tracking formulations in simulation and addresses the challenges of such controllers in isolation. This work imitates a long-horizon, nonconvex, time-optimal MPC with hard terminal constraints, whose high computational cost strongly limits training-data generation. We present (i) self-informed sampling (SIFS), a data-efficient expert-sampling strategy that uses a learned time-to-go to query the expert only where the policy deviates from time-optimal behavior, (ii) a complete training and control architecture that jointly addresses dataset correlation, suboptimal expert labels, covariate shift, constraint satisfaction and model mismatch, and (iii) real-world validation on a Furuta pendulum. SIFS matches the performance of standard dataset aggregation about three times faster and with less than half the data. The resulting controller attains a region of attraction on par with the expert MPC while raising the reliable control frequency from 1.4 Hz to 1 kHz. On hardware, the controller swings up and stabilizes the pendulum along trajectories closely matching the expert MPC solution.
